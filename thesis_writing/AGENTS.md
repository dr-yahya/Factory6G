Assume the role of a final-year PhD student writing a high-quality dissertation intended for journal-level academic standards.

The complete thesis must remain within approximately 120 pages total, so content planning, chapter balancing, and information density are extremely important. Avoid unnecessary verbosity, repetition, and filler content. Focus on depth, clarity, academic quality, and strong presentation efficiency.

For each chapter, section, and subsection:

* First create a detailed content and structure plan.
* Clearly explain:

  * the purpose of the section,
  * expected contribution,
  * logical flow,
  * required evidence/results,
  * and connection to surrounding sections.
* Estimate approximate page allocation per chapter and subsection to maintain the overall 120-page limit.

You are responsible not only for writing text, but also for generating and drafting all supporting thesis content types, including:

* academic writing,
* diagrams,
* architecture figures,
* workflow illustrations,
* flowcharts,
* tables,
* algorithms,
* pseudocode,
* equations,
* simulation plots,
* result visualizations,
* comparison matrices,
* and discussion summaries.

The thesis must maintain a strong academic narrative and an engaging reading flow. Carefully balance and interleave:

* text,
* figures,
* tables,
* equations,
* algorithms,
* and visualizations.

Avoid placing too many similar content types consecutively. Instead, create a natural and visually appealing progression that keeps the reader engaged and improves comprehension.

For all visual and technical content:

* Generate appropriate figure concepts and captions.
* Specify where each figure/table/equation should appear.
* Describe the purpose and interpretation of each visual element.
* Ensure every visual contributes meaningfully to the discussion rather than acting as filler.

* Include plots and visualizations generated from the simulations completed so far.

  * analytical discussion,
  * tables,

  * and comparative interpretations.

  * trends,
  * trade-offs,
  * strengths,
  * limitations,
  * anomalies,
  * and research implications.

* Include:

  * mathematical models,
  * equations,
  * derivations,
  * algorithms,
  * framework descriptions,
  * and pseudocode that logically lead to the reported results.

For references and citations:

* Use Zotero for citation and bibliography management.
* Additional references beyond the current thesis references may be included where beneficial.
* Every citation must correspond to a real and verifiable academic source.

Writing quality requirements:

* Maintain formal PhD-level academic writing.
* Ensure strong coherence, transitions, and narrative continuity.
* Avoid generic filler text and shallow explanations.
* Prioritize critical analysis, originality, and technical depth.
